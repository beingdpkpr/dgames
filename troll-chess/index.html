How to play

Normal chess moves. Before every turn you draw a card from a shared deck, and the card is your turn.

    Winning. Capture the enemy king, or checkmate it: if a player draws a number card while in check and has no legal move, they lose. A player left in check who draws Skip, Reverse, Draw 2 or Wild cannot answer it, and the king can simply be taken next turn.

    Check ends your turn. A move that puts the enemy king in check uses up the rest of a 2 or 3, so the opponent always gets a card to answer with. You may end a 2 or 3 early after one move. With no legal move and no check, the turn just passes.

    The deck is 40 cards and reshuffles when it runs out. A captured piece that had been promoted comes back as a pawn. Castling, en passant and promotion work as usual; a king or rook that was revived or moved by a Wild loses its castling right.

    Three modes. Card Chess is the cards above with ordinary pieces. Fusion Chess has no cards: one move a turn with the fusion pieces below; checkmate wins as in chess, and 50 moves each with no capture and no pawn move is a draw. Card + Fusion (the default) is both at once.

    Fusion pieces

    • KnookA knight and a rook on one square, by moving either onto the other. Moves like both.
    • KnishopA knight and a bishop, the same way. Moves like both.
    • KningThe king steps onto its knight (or the knight jumps onto the king). Moves one or two squares along any king line, and counts as the king for check, except that checking it does not end your turn: with a 2 or 3 you can check it and keep going. If it is taken, only the knight is lost: the king goes to the nearest safe free square within two squares, and is taken only if there is none. So a Kning in check with no move is not mated; the turn just passes.

    Captured, a Knook or Knishop goes back to the pool as its two pieces. Draw 2 can revive a knight onto your rook or bishop in the revive quarter, or onto your king wherever it is: the one way a Draw 2 can shield a king in check. Wild can drop a knight onto your rook, bishop or king anywhere. Neither may put the enemy king in check. A pawn placed by a Wild next to an enemy pawn on the same rank can be taken en passant on the next move.
