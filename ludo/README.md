# Ludo

Classic Indian Ludo for two to four players on one device. Seat 1 is you; every other seat is a human or a computer at Easy, Normal or Hard, the same way Dots & Boxes does it.

Open `index.html` directly. No dependencies, nothing fetched from the network.

Controls: tap the die to roll, then tap a highlighted token or one of the move buttons under the board. When there is only one legal move it plays itself after a beat. Everything is tap-driven, so the phone and the desktop play the same way.

## Rules implemented

- Four tokens each, all starting in the yard.
- A six is needed to bring a token out; it lands on that player's own start square.
- A six grants another turn.
- Landing on a single opponent token sends it back to its yard and grants another turn.
- The eight safe squares — the four start squares and the four stars eight ahead of them — cannot be captured on. Tokens of different players share them.
- Two tokens of one player on a square form a block. Opponents can neither land on it nor pass through it; the owner moves off it freely.
- Three sixes in a row forfeits the turn, and the third six is not played.
- Home needs the exact count. An overshoot is not a legal move, so a token can be stuck one square short.
- A roll with no legal move passes the turn.
- The first player to get all four tokens home wins.

## Board

A token's position is one number, its progress along its own path: `-1` in the yard, `0..50` on the shared 52-square ring, `51..55` in its own five-square home run, `56` home. A lap is 51 ring squares rather than 52, because the 52nd square of the loop is the one the player turns off at. The ring itself is generated by rotating a single 13-square arm four times; the track steps diagonally at each of the four corners, since the corner square belongs to the centre triangle.

## Computer levels

Easy plays any legal move. Normal plays the value up: capture, then reach home, then bring a token out, then reach safety, then advance the leader. Hard adds the one thing Normal ignores — it will not park on a square an opponent can reach on their next roll.

The difference is small on purpose, because Ludo is a dice game. Measured over 1200 seeded games with the seats swapped half way, Hard beats Normal 55.7% of the time (±1.4), and both beat Easy about nine times in ten.

## Record

There is no high-score table here — Ludo has no score to rank. Instead a win/loss record and an unbeaten streak are kept per table size, so a two-player record is not mixed with a four-player one. Stored in this browser's localStorage under `dgames.ludo.record`; options live under `dgames.ludo.opts`. Both are per browser and per device, and clearing site data removes them.

## Tests

`node test/rules.js` loads the pure rules section out of `index.html` and drives exact scenarios — a capture, a block that cannot be passed, a third six, an overshoot at home — which is only possible because the engine never rolls its own dice: every call takes the roll as an argument. It also checks the board geometry closes up and that a full seeded game finishes.

`node test/ai.js` plays seeded level-vs-level matches with the seats swapped half way and prints each win rate with its standard error, so a regression shows up as a number rather than a pass/fail flip.

`node test/record.js` checks the win/loss/streak store round-trips, keeps table sizes apart, and reads as blank when localStorage holds junk.
